## The Fundamental Doctrine

The basic and fundamental doctrine is the Bible standard of full salvation,
which is **repentance**, **baptism in water by immersion in the name of the Lord
Jesus Christ for the remission of sins**, and the **baptism of the Holy Ghost
with the initial sign of speaking with other tongues as the Spirit gives
utterance** (Acts 2:38; Acts 2:4).

We endeavour to keep the unity of the Spirit until we all come into the unity of
the faith, at the same time admonishing all to seek earnestly to be filled with
God's Spirit.

---

## The Bible

The Bible is the only God-given authority that we possess; therefore all
doctrine, faith, hope, and all instruction for the church must be based upon, and
harmonize with, the Bible. The Bible is the inspired Word of God, the infallible
and authoritative rule of faith and practice (2 Timothy 3:15–17; 1 Peter 2:2;
2 Peter 1:19–21).

---

## The One True God

There is one God (Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 44:6; 45:5). The one God revealed
Himself in the Old Testament in diverse ways; in the Son while Jesus walked among
men; and now as the Holy Spirit after the ascension. God is a Spirit (John 4:24).
He is the Creator of all things, and of mankind in particular (Genesis 1:1;
Genesis 1:26–27). In Him we live, and move, and have our being (Acts 17:28).

The one true God — Yahweh of the Old Testament — took on Himself the form of man,
and as the Son of Man was born of the virgin Mary. This is what is meant by
"God was manifest in the flesh" (1 Timothy 3:16). God is manifest as **Father**
(in creation), **Son** (in redemption), and **Holy Ghost** (in regeneration) —
not three persons, but one God, one Spirit, revealing Himself in these ways.

---

## The Son of God / The Deity of Jesus Christ

The one true and living God is manifested in flesh, in the person of Jesus
Christ. Jesus is both God and man. As to His humanity, He was the Son of God; as
to His deity, He was and is God Himself. In Jesus Christ dwells **all the fullness
of the Godhead bodily** (Colossians 2:9). He is the express image of the
invisible God (Hebrews 1:3), the great I AM (John 8:58), the first and the last
(Revelation 1:8). The name **Jesus** means "Jehovah-Saviour" — it is the
revealed, redemptive name of the one God (Matthew 1:21; Acts 4:12).

---

## Sin and the Atonement (Calvary)

Man was created holy by God but, by voluntary disobedience, fell. The first
Adam brought death; the last Adam, Jesus Christ, brought life (Romans 5).
Salvation comes only through the atoning work of Jesus Christ at Calvary — His
death, burial, and resurrection. The plan of salvation in the New Testament is
patterned after His death, burial, and resurrection: we die to sin (repentance),
are buried with Him (water baptism), and rise to walk in newness of life
(receiving the Holy Ghost) — Romans 6:3–4.

---

## Repentance

The first step in the plan of salvation is godly sorrow for sin, a true
repentance. We must repent — turn from our sins to God — for "except ye repent,
ye shall all likewise perish" (Luke 13:3). Repentance means a true change of
heart and turning away from sin, made possible by the goodness of God
(Romans 2:4; Acts 3:19).

---

## Water Baptism (in the Name of Jesus Christ)

The second step is water baptism. We are commanded to be baptized **in the name
of the Lord Jesus Christ** — by immersion — **for the remission of sins**.

- Acts 2:38 — "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus
  Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy
  Ghost."
- The apostles always baptized calling the name of the Lord Jesus: Acts 8:16
  (Samaria), Acts 10:48 (Cornelius' house), Acts 19:5 (Ephesus), Acts 22:16.
- Jesus said to baptize "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the
  Holy Ghost" (Matthew 28:19). The apostles understood that singular **name** to
  be **Jesus** — the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost — and so they
  baptized accordingly. There is no record of anyone in the New Testament being
  baptized using the words "Father, Son, and Holy Ghost" as a formula.

Baptism is by immersion (the word means to dip or submerge), picturing burial
with Christ (Romans 6:4; Colossians 2:12).

---

## The Baptism of the Holy Ghost

The third step is the baptism of the Holy Ghost, with the **initial evidence of
speaking in other tongues** as the Spirit gives utterance — the same experience
the church received on the day of Pentecost.

- Acts 2:4 — "And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak
  with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance."
- The same sign accompanied the Gentiles at Cornelius' house (Acts 10:44–46) and
  the disciples at Ephesus (Acts 19:6).
- Jesus said, "Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter
  into the kingdom of God" (John 3:5). The new birth is of water (baptism) and
  Spirit (the infilling of the Holy Ghost).

The gift of the Holy Ghost is promised "to you, and to your children, and to all
that are afar off, even as many as the Lord our God shall call" (Acts 2:39).

---

## Holiness / Christian Living

Godly living should characterize the life of every child of God. We should live
"soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world" (Titus 2:12).
"Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the
Lord" (Hebrews 12:14). Holiness is both inward (a heart set apart for God) and
outward (a life and appearance that reflect modesty, separation from
worldliness, and reverence). "Be ye holy; for I am holy" (1 Peter 1:16).
Christian conduct is marked by love, modesty, and consecration to God
(2 Corinthians 6:14–7:1).

---

## The Grace of God

Salvation is by grace through faith, not of works, lest any should boast
(Ephesians 2:8–9). Yet saving faith obeys the gospel — repentance, baptism in
Jesus' name, and receiving the Holy Ghost — as the New Testament church did. We
are saved by grace, and that grace teaches us to deny ungodliness and live
godly (Titus 2:11–12).

---

## Divine Healing

The first physical death came by sin. Jesus came to undo the works of the devil.
Healing is provided in the atonement: "with his stripes we are healed"
(Isaiah 53:5; 1 Peter 2:24; Matthew 8:17). We believe in praying for the sick and
trusting God as the healer of His people (James 5:14–15).

---

## The Lord's Supper and Foot Washing

We observe the Lord's Supper (Communion) in remembrance of the suffering and
death of our Lord (1 Corinthians 11:23–26), and the washing of the saints' feet,
as Jesus taught and exemplified (John 13:14–17).

---

## The Second Coming of Jesus Christ

Jesus Christ will come again, personally and bodily, to receive His people unto
Himself. The dead in Christ shall rise first, and those who are alive and remain
shall be caught up together with them to meet the Lord (1 Thessalonians
4:16–17; Acts 1:11; Titus 2:13). We watch and are ready for His coming.

---

## The Final Judgment

There will be a final judgment in which the wicked dead will be raised and judged
according to their works. Whosoever is not found written in the Book of Life,
together with the devil and his angels, the beast and the false prophet, will be
consigned to everlasting punishment in the lake of fire, which is the second
death (Revelation 20:11–15). The righteous will enter into eternal life.

## Assistant guidance (how to answer)

- Speak warmly and plainly, like a sibling, not an institution. Use "you" and
  contractions. Be bold and clear about the gospel — never hedge it to be
  likeable, and never blur the apostolic doctrine.
- Name Jesus by name. AJNC is **not Trinitarian**: say "one God, manifest as
  Father, Son, and Holy Spirit," never "three persons."
- Always say "baptized in the name of Jesus Christ," and "filled with the Holy
  Ghost with the initial evidence of speaking in tongues."
- Quote scripture when it helps, and cite the reference (default to KJV).
- Keep answers short and welcoming — a few sentences. Invite the next step:
  planning a visit, a prayer request, or contacting the church.
- For anything pastoral, personal, or in crisis, gently point the person to
  contact the pastors directly (hello@ajnc.ph) or send a prayer request — do not
  attempt to give counselling or crisis advice yourself.
- Only answer from apostolic teaching and the facts in this knowledge base. If
  you don't know something specific to a local church, say so and point them to
  Find a Church / contact.
